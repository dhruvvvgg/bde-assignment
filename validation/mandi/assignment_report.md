# Clustering: Indian mandi price-behavior groups

## 1. Problem statement
Group Onion, Potato and Tomato market/variety/grade profiles by observed price variability over 2022-01-01 to 2024-12-31. Farmer producer organizations could prioritize markets for monitoring. Each commodity is modeled separately, preventing commodity identity from driving the groups.

## 2. Model and justification
K-means is fast and easy to explain on standardized numeric behavior profiles. Choose 2–6 groups by highest silhouette among solutions meeting minimum size and iteration checks. Price CV, consecutive-day return volatility, sharp-drop frequency, monthly variation and available relative spread describe observed behavior. Absolute price is excluded from the main model. K-means is established, not a novel algorithm. Classification accuracy and specificity are inapplicable without true cluster labels.

## 3. Coding
All code is embedded in the Kaggle notebook. Year files are scanned once in CSV chunks or Parquet batches and all three commodities are filtered together. Subsequent profiles, clipping, scaling and K-means fitting are separate by commodity. Known price inconsistencies and duplicates are removed; daily medians resolve multiple reports. Common-window coverage and consecutive-calendar-day returns are enforced. Stale reporting, extreme returns and variety switches are flagged.

## 4. Results
Raw records scanned once: 14,437,080; retained unique commodity records: 1,829,255; eligible profiles: 1,750. Per-commodity tables/charts report cluster-count comparisons, median behavior, geometric separation, stability, clipping/price sensitivity and representative histories.

## 5. Inference and what-if analysis
**Onion:** selected 2 K-means groups, silhouette 0.441, Davies–Bouldin 1.136 and mean subsampling ARI 0.893. Group 0 contains 153 profiles; median daily volatility 0.147, median sharp-drop frequency 13.2%. Group 1 contains 453 profiles; median daily volatility 0.078, median sharp-drop frequency 3.9%. Assignment ARI without clipping is 0.708; with 2% clipping it is 0.853. Low values reveal dependence on extreme-value treatment. Adding price level changes assignments with ARI 0.924. Profiles with non-positive silhouettes are flagged for weak geometric separation; these scores are not membership probabilities or future price risk.

**Potato:** selected 2 K-means groups, silhouette 0.383, Davies–Bouldin 1.277 and mean subsampling ARI 0.988. Group 0 contains 430 profiles; median daily volatility 0.062, median sharp-drop frequency 2.6%. Group 1 contains 146 profiles; median daily volatility 0.155, median sharp-drop frequency 13.3%. Assignment ARI without clipping is -0.046; with 2% clipping it is 0.985. Low values reveal dependence on extreme-value treatment. Adding price level changes assignments with ARI 0.852. Profiles with non-positive silhouettes are flagged for weak geometric separation; these scores are not membership probabilities or future price risk.

**Tomato:** selected 2 K-means groups, silhouette 0.368, Davies–Bouldin 1.220 and mean subsampling ARI 0.942. Group 0 contains 415 profiles; median daily volatility 0.133, median sharp-drop frequency 10.0%. Group 1 contains 153 profiles; median daily volatility 0.247, median sharp-drop frequency 21.9%. Assignment ARI without clipping is 0.842; with 2% clipping it is 0.993. Low values reveal dependence on extreme-value treatment. Adding price level changes assignments with ARI 0.845. Profiles with non-positive silhouettes are flagged for weak geometric separation; these scores are not membership probabilities or future price risk.

Each commodity was preprocessed and modeled separately, so group numbers are not comparable across commodities. Higher median volatility/drop-frequency groups may warrant closer monitoring. Monthly variation is a proxy, not proof of seasonality; reports can reflect missing markets, variety recoding and extreme movements. Drop/coverage/profile-separation what-if tables are descriptive, not forecasts. Median summaries resist extreme-value inflation. Cluster count is selected by highest silhouette among solutions satisfying minimum size and iteration checks; Davies–Bouldin, Calinski–Harabasz and inertia are supporting diagnostics. ARI stability holds preprocessing fixed and does not establish economic validity. Transport costs, quantities and farmer-level selling prices are absent.
